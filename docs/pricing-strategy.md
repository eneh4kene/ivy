1. Product Overview
Ivy is an AI accountability partner that calls users daily — morning and evening — to build and sustain positive habits. Unlike passive fitness apps with a 96% abandonment rate, Ivy creates a commitment loop through real phone calls that cannot be swiped away.

Every time a user follows through on their commitment, money from their Impact Wallet is donated to a charity of their choice. Consistency becomes meaningful — not just personal achievement, but real-world impact.

On habit-to-track assignment
Some habits naturally span multiple tracks depending on the user's intent.
Example: reading. If the goal is learning and cognitive output, it belongs in Focus.
If the goal is relaxation or winding down before sleep, it belongs in Balance or Sleep.
Ivy lets users self-assign habits to the track that best reflects their intent.
The track determines the tone and framing of Ivy's check-in call — not a rigid category.
This keeps the system flexible as users build unusual or highly personal habits.


Fitness is the lead wedge for MVP and initial pilots — it has the clearest yes/no verification and the broadest market appeal. All other tracks follow the same call structure and mechanics.

Seasons and Sprints — how they work
Ivy organises a user's journey into two nested time structures:

Sprint — the 4-week execution unit. Each sprint ends with an Ivy Circle session (the checkpoint meeting). Within a sprint, the daily call loop runs as normal: morning commitment, evening close, rescue calls available, wallet firing on follow-through.

Season — the overarching goal arc. A season is approximately 12 weeks (3 sprints) by default, though configurable. A season has a single overarching goal — something meaningful enough to take 3 months to achieve. It contains 3 sprints, each with its own sprint pledge set at the end of the Ivy Circle session.

This distinction matters:
Sprint = accountability unit. Did you show up this week? This month?
Season = transformation unit. Who are you becoming over 3 months?

The sprint is where the daily loop lives. The season is where the story lives.

At the end of each sprint, the Ivy Circle session reviews that sprint and sets the pledge for the next. At the end of the season, something different happens — the Season Close (see Section 3a).



2. The Impact Wallet
The Impact Wallet is the charitable giving mechanic at the heart of Ivy's value proposition. It replaces hollow gamification with genuine external meaning — your consistency funds real-world change.

Core Principles
Money only moves on follow-through. Missing a day fires nothing — the model is purely positive reinforcement.
We never profit from the wallet. 100% of wallet funds pass through to charity. Ivy's revenue comes entirely from subscriptions.
Wallet funding is built into the subscription price at every paid tier. Users are not paying extra to give.
Full source transparency. Users see exactly where each contribution came from (subscription allocation, employer, individual supporter).
Supporter top-ups are opt-in public. Friends, family, or followers can top up a user's wallet as encouragement — essentially betting on their follow-through.
Streak bonuses are universal. All paid tiers unlock bonus donations at 7, 30, and 90-day consistency milestones. Consistency should always be celebrated — gating streak bonuses would punish the behaviour the product is built to reward.

3. Ivy Circles — Sprint Cohort Coaching
Ivy Circles is the structured group accountability feature available from Ivy Plus and Concierge tiers. It solves the isolation problem — Ivy holds users accountable daily, but Circles makes the journey social, witnessed, and coached.

Circles happen at the end of every sprint — three times per season. They are not the same as the Season Close. Each Circle reviews the sprint just completed and sets the sprint pledge for the next. The Season Close (Section 3a) is a separate, larger moment at the end of the full season arc.

Sprint Structure
Sprint length: 4 weeks (3 sprints per season).
Group size: 6-8 people (Ivy Plus, peer-facilitated) or max 4 (Ivy Concierge, pro/celebrity coach).
Frequency: Once per sprint — three sessions across a 12-week season.

Cohort Grouping Criteria
Track alignment: Fitness users with Fitness users, Focus with Focus, etc.
Consistency score: Similar Ivy follow-through rates grouped together (prevents demotivation gaps).
Sprint number: Users in their first sprint stay together. By Sprint 3 they know each other — bonds form.

3a. Season Close — The Most Important Moment in the Product

The Season Close is a single ceremonial call that happens at the end of every 12-week season. It is not a rest period, not a gap, and not a passive summary email. It is a deliberate, designed event that marks the end of one chapter and the beginning of the next.

This is the highest-value moment in Ivy's retention architecture. The user has 12 weeks of evidence that they can show up. The charity impact is at its most tangible. The emotional investment is at its peak. This is the moment to honour the journey and earn the next season's commitment.

What happens in the Season Close call
Ivy walks the user through their arc — not just stats, but the story. Sprint by sprint: where they started, where they struggled, where they broke through. The call surfaces moments the user may have forgotten: a life marker they logged in week 7, a rescue call they made instead of quitting, the sprint where everything clicked.

Ivy then presents the full-season charity impact — the cumulative wallet total, the streak bonus milestones hit, and (if available) a season-level impact story from the charity. This is the most powerful version of the Impact Stories feature: not a sprint note, but the whole arc.

Then one question: "What do you want your next season to be for?"

Goal suggestion
Ivy does not ask this question cold. Based on 12 weeks of call transcripts, follow-through data by day and sprint, life markers, energy and mood scores, and the user's own language across calls, Ivy generates 2-3 suggested goals for the next season. These are personalised — not generic prompts, but specific observations:

"You started on Fitness and hit 91% in your last sprint. You mentioned wanting to reduce stress twice in calls. One direction: keep Fitness as your anchor and add a Balance track for the new season."
"Your consistency was highest in weeks where your morning call was before 7am. Your next season goal could be building an earlier morning routine as the foundation."
"You've completed one full season. Many members at this stage set a larger goal — something they'd have felt too ambitious to say on day one."

The user picks one of Ivy's suggestions, modifies it, or sets their own. Either way, Ivy records it as the season goal and builds the first sprint pledge around it.

On rest between seasons
A forced gap between seasons carries real risk — streaks break, habits fade, and the product disappears from the user's life for a week. The Season Close call is the pause. It creates breathing room within a single call rather than a dead week between seasons.

For users who want more space, the transition is opt-in. After the Season Close call, Ivy asks: "Ready to start your next season on Monday, or would you like a week to settle first?" If they choose a rest week, Ivy does not go silent. She shifts to lighter check-ins — not accountability, just presence — so the relationship holds and the streak context remains intact.

Season Close as a commercial moment
This moment is also the natural point for:
Upgrade suggestion — data-driven, not pushy. "You hit 87% consistency this season. Ivy Circles is designed for people at exactly this stage." Said after 12 weeks of evidence, this lands differently than any marketing message.
Annual plan offer — "You've completed your first season. Committing to a full year means your next three seasons are locked in. Many members find that removes the renewal friction and lets them focus on the goal."
Tier upgrade — if a Concierge user's season suggests they'd benefit from a certified coach rather than a peer coach, the Season Close is where that conversation makes sense.

None of these are hard sells. They are natural next steps surfaced at the moment the user is most engaged, most proud, and most likely to invest further.


4. Ivy Impact Stories
Most charitable giving is abstract. You donate, money moves, and you never feel it land. Ivy Impact Stories closes that loop. Every sprint, members who hit their consistency threshold receive a personalised message from the charity their wallet funded, acknowledging their contribution by name.

This transforms a passive donation into a felt relationship. A voice saying your name is not forgettable. That moment becomes the emotional anchor that drives renewal, upgrades, and word-of-mouth.

The core idea
You follow through for 4 weeks. Money moves to your chosen charity.
At the end of the sprint, someone at that charity says thank you - specifically to you.
Not a newsletter. Not a generic receipt. Something with your name on it.
This is the moment that makes Ivy feel different to anything else in this space.

Operational Model
Ivy cannot manually coordinate personalised content with every charity for every member. Two models handle this at scale:

Model A - Charity Content Library (preferred)
As part of Ivy charity onboarding, charities agree to provide a content library: a bank of thank you notes, photos, audio clips, and video messages.
Ivy personalises the content at delivery - inserting the member name, consistency score, wallet amount, and impact data before sending.
The charity provides content once. Ivy handles personalisation and delivery at scale.
Content library refreshed quarterly - new stories, new faces, new moments.

Model B - Ivy-Produced Impact Updates (fallback)
For charities that cannot produce content, Ivy creates templated impact updates using the charity published data and branding.
Personalised with member name, consistency stats, and wallet contribution amount.
Lower emotional resonance than a real voice or video - but far more meaningful than a generic donation receipt.
Used as a bridge while the charity relationship matures toward Model A.

Authenticity is Non-Negotiable
Audio and video must feature real beneficiaries or coordinators - never AI-generated voices or stock footage.
Notes must reference specific, verifiable impact tied to the member wallet amount - not generic charity copy.
Concierge-tier video content must be genuinely personalised - a sign with the member name, a direct address, something that could not have been sent to anyone else.
Ivy audits charity content libraries quarterly to ensure quality and freshness.

Viral Potential
A member who receives a video of a child holding a sign with their name is likely to share it. That organic moment is more powerful than any paid marketing campaign. Impact Stories should be designed with shareability in mind: one-tap sharing to social, a clean Ivy-branded frame, and a caption that tells the story without requiring explanation.

9. Strategic Notes
Pricing Philosophy — Full AI, Human as Differentiator
Every paid tier includes the complete AI experience. No rescue calls, tracks, or features are gated behind higher tiers.
This eliminates broken lower tiers — a Core user who can't call Ivy when about to skip is a churn event caused by the pricing structure, not the product.
The two honest differentiators are: (1) wallet size — more money to charity — and (2) human involvement quality — peer coach vs. certified pro vs. celebrity.
Human involvement is naturally scarce and expensive — it is a genuine differentiator, not an artificial one.
The one-liner: "Every Ivy plan gives you the full AI experience. What you are choosing is how much impact you want to create and how much human coaching you want alongside it."

14-Day Free Trial Rationale
Ivy's value proposition is the daily call. A permanently crippled free tier (no calls) misrepresents the product and creates negative first impressions.
A 14-day trial with full Core experience — real calls, real wallet, real Ivy — converts far better than a feature-limited freemium tier.
"Try Ivy free for 14 days. No card required. Real calls, real impact from day one." is a cleaner, more premium pitch.

Positioning vs. Competitors
vs. fitness apps (MyFitnessPal, Strava): Ivy is not a tracking app. It is an accountability layer that sits on top of any routine.
vs. human coaches (£200-500/mo): Ivy delivers comparable accountability at £70-149/mo without judgment anxiety.
vs. accountability buddies: Ivy never flakes, never judges, and is available at 6am or 11pm.

Coach Marketplace — Long-Term Play
Peer coaches (Ivy Graduates) cost nothing — compensated in wallet credits that stay in the charitable ecosystem.
Pro coaches earn ~£6/user/sprint for group sessions via marketplace. At 4 groups of 8, that's £192/mo for ~4 hours' work — compelling enough to attract quality coaches.
Celebrity coaches are recruited as charitable brand partnerships, not employees. The pitch: "I coach 8 people a month for Ivy and it funds clean water in Kenya."
Ivy takes 15-20% of all marketplace bookings (additional 1-on-1s beyond the included quarterly session) — a margin-accretive revenue stream at scale.

B2B Entry Strategy
Target SMEs (20-100 employees) first — HR budgets are more flexible, decision cycles are shorter, and they're easier to pilot.
The pitch: "Your wellness budget becomes measurable real-world impact." Charity impact reports are a powerful differentiator vs. generic EAP programmes.
Each B2B client is a cohort factory — 50 employees = 6-7 Ivy Circles groups per sprint, with three sprints per season generating a rich arc of data and social proof.

Retell Infrastructure
Ivy uses Retell AI + Twilio for voice, running on Fly.io (Docker, lhr region) with a Node.js/Express backend and Neon PostgreSQL. This infrastructure is already live and proven.

⚠️ Coach B2B Tier — Unit Economics Warning
The coach-facing B2B tier (separate from Sponsor Mode above) has broken unit economics at current pricing. Each client on a coach plan generates ~£30/mo in voice COGS (2 calls/day × ~£0.50/call × 30 days), but coaches pay a flat fee that doesn't cover this at any tier. Do not scale or market the coach tier aggressively before the pricing model is fixed. Three viable paths:
1. Cap clients per tier + charge per-client overage (e.g. Starter = 3 clients, £8/extra)
2. Reduce coach clients to 1 call/day (evening only) instead of 2
3. Charge coaches per-client (e.g. £12/client/month) — aligns incentives, scales correctly
