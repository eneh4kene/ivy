1. Product Overview
Ivy is an AI accountability partner that calls users daily — morning and evening — to build and sustain positive habits. Unlike passive fitness apps with a 96% abandonment rate, Ivy creates a commitment loop through real phone calls that cannot be swiped away.

Every time a user follows through on their commitment, money from their Impact Wallet is donated to a charity of their choice. Consistency becomes meaningful — not just personal achievement, but real-world impact.

On habit-to-track assignment
Some habits naturally span multiple tracks depending on the user's intent.
Example: reading. If the goal is learning and cognitive output, it belongs in Focus.
If the goal is relaxation or winding down before sleep, it belongs in Balance or Sleep.
Ivy lets users self-assign habits to the track that best reflects their intent.
The track determines the tone and framing of Ivy's check-in call — not a rigid category.
This keeps the system flexible as users build unusual or highly personal habits.


Fitness is the lead wedge for MVP and initial pilots — it has the clearest yes/no verification and the broadest market appeal. All other tracks follow the same call structure and mechanics.

Core Product Mechanics
Daily morning call: Ivy asks "What's your plan today?" — sets the commitment.
Daily evening call: Ivy asks "Did you do it?" — closes the loop.
Rescue calls: User-initiated when about to skip. Ivy negotiates a minimum that still counts.

Impact Wallet: Micro-donations fire on every confirmed follow-through.
Ivy Circles: Monthly sprint cohort sessions with a coach — available from Ivy Plus and Concierge tiers.



2. The Impact Wallet
The Impact Wallet is the charitable giving mechanic at the heart of Ivy's value proposition. It replaces hollow gamification with genuine external meaning — your consistency funds real-world change.

Core Principles
Money only moves on follow-through. Missing a day fires nothing — the model is purely positive reinforcement.
We never profit from the wallet. 100% of wallet funds pass through to charity. Ivy's revenue comes entirely from subscriptions.
Wallet funding is built into the subscription price at every paid tier. Users are not paying extra to give.
Full source transparency. Users see exactly where each contribution came from (subscription allocation, employer, individual supporter).
Supporter top-ups are opt-in public. Friends, family, or followers can top up a user's wallet as encouragement — essentially betting on their follow-through.

3. Season Structure — The Ivy Time Architecture

What a Season Looks Like
A season is a 12-week transformation arc anchored to a specific, meaningful goal. Examples:

Run a half marathon
Lose 2 stone
Meditate every day for a season
Read 12 books
Build a consistent sleep schedule

The goal is set at season start during onboarding. Ivy uses it to frame every daily call, every sprint review, and the Season Close. The goal is not a rigid target — it is a direction. Users who miss their exact goal but show up consistently still complete a season.

Sprint as the Execution Layer
Each season contains exactly 3 sprints of 4 weeks each.
Each sprint has a focused sub-goal that ladders up to the season goal.
At the end of each sprint, Ivy Plus and Concierge users have an Ivy Circle session — their cohort reviews progress, celebrates wins, surfaces struggles, and sets the next sprint commitment.
Sprint-end Impact Stories fire here — the charity acknowledgement that closes each 4-week arc.

The Season Close
The Season Close is a ceremonial AI call that runs at the end of week 12. It is one of Ivy's most important product moments — commercially, emotionally, and for retention.

Ivy walks the user through their full 12-week arc — consistency score, energy trends, mood patterns, life markers, transformation data.
Every completed day is acknowledged. Every rescue call that saved a streak is surfaced.
The total wallet amount donated across the season is revealed — the cumulative charity impact of 12 weeks of follow-through.
Ivy celebrates what was achieved without penalising what was not.
Ivy suggests a goal for Season 2 based on the user's data and trajectory.
The Season Close is the natural moment to offer an upgrade, an annual plan, or a new season start.

The Season Close as a commercial moment
The Season Close is a built-in renewal conversation that does not feel like a sales pitch.
The user has just heard their own transformation story told back to them.
They are at peak emotional engagement with Ivy.
The ask — start Season 2, upgrade to Plus, switch to an annual plan — lands in that moment.
No other wellness product has a structured moment like this.


Why Seasons Change the Subscription Dynamic
Seasons reframe the purchase decision. Instead of subscribing to Ivy, users start a season. That is a defined commitment with a beginning, middle, and end — far easier to say yes to than an open-ended rolling subscription.
Seasons create a natural renewal moment. At Season Close, the user consciously chooses to start Season 2. This is active re-commitment, not passive inertia. It produces higher-quality retained users.
Seasons build shared identity. Users say I am in Season 2 the way people say they are in their second year of something. This powers the Ivy Circles social layer — cohorts that survive a full season together have been through something real.
Seasons map to how humans think about change. People think in chapters, not forever. A season has the emotional weight of a chapter. An open-ended subscription does not.

Mid-Season Joins
Users who join mid-season face a design decision: join the active season in progress, or wait for the next season start. The recommended approach:

Users who join mid-sprint are onboarded immediately on a rolling basis — they start their daily calls on day one and join an active sprint already in progress.
Their first full season starts at the next season boundary. Their introductory period (however many weeks remain in the current sprint) is treated as a pre-season warmup.
This minimises friction at signup while preserving the integrity of the season structure for cohort matching and Ivy Circle scheduling.



4. Season Activities
Season Activities are optional, time-limited group challenges that run alongside the personal accountability loop. They are the social and viral layer of Ivy — designed to make users pull friends in, talk about Ivy publicly, and feel genuine emotional stakes beyond their personal habit journey.

Three design principles for all Season Activities
1. Participation requires accountability — you can only join an activity if you are on an active streak. Fun is tied directly to the core habit loop.
2. Activities are track-relevant — Fitness activities involve movement, Focus activities involve deep work, and so on.
3. Activities are shareable — every activity has a moment designed to be posted. The output is content, not just an experience.


Activity Concept 1 — The Baton
A team relay challenge. A group of 5-8 users collectively completes a distance, duration, or rep target in a rolling 7-day window. Each person holds the baton for a defined time window and must contribute before passing it on.

Team sets a collective goal (e.g. 50km across 6 people in a week).
Each person has a rolling time window to hold the baton — typically 4 hours.
When your window opens, Ivy calls: you. Your window is open. What are you contributing?
You log your contribution. Ivy passes the baton to the next person automatically.
Miss your window and the baton drops — the team loses their streak.

The viral moment: the baton drop notification. Sarah just dropped the baton. 4 hours left to save the team. That gets posted.

Activity Concept 2 — The Impostor (Circle Game Mode)
An Among Us-inspired mechanic, explicitly positioned as a separate party game mode — not integrated into the core accountability Circles session. This distinction is important for brand integrity: Ivy is built on follow-through and honesty. The Impostor works as a clearly labelled opt-in game night format, not as a feature of the daily habit loop.

How it works as a game mode:
A group of 8-10 users opt in to a standalone Circle Game session — separate from their regular sprint Circles.
Ivy secretly assigns one player as the Impostor. Their task: convince the group they completed a fictional habit challenge.
Everyone else argues their case. The group votes. Catch the Impostor and the group wins a wallet bonus. Survive undetected and the Impostor wins.
Crucially — no real habit completions are affected. The game session is ring-fenced from accountability data.

Why it still builds accountability: articulating habits in vivid, convincing detail — even fictionally — sharpens users awareness of what genuine follow-through looks, sounds, and feels like. The paranoia and social dynamic make the session electric.

Brand boundary — why this must stay separate
Ivy is built on accountability and follow-through. That trust is the product.
The Impostor must never be integrated into the core Circles session or daily call loop.
It lives in a clearly labelled game mode — fun, opt-in, ring-fenced.
The headline we want: Ivy makes accountability fun.
The headline we must avoid: Ivy helps users fake their habits.


Activity Concept 3 — The Relay Season
A cross-user season challenge where each Circle member carries the team goal for one week before handing off to the next person.

The Circle sets a collective season goal (e.g. combined 300 workouts in 12 weeks).
Each week one person is the Pace Setter — their consistency counts double toward the team total.
The Pace Setter role rotates weekly so everyone leads once per sprint.
Ivy calls the Pace Setter with heightened urgency: you are carrying the team this week.

The social dynamic: no one wants to be the person who tanked the team's week when it was their turn. The Pace Setter week becomes the most motivated week of any user's season.

Activity Concept 4 — Season Rivals
Two Circles from the same cohort compete across a full season on collective consistency score.

Two groups of 6 are matched at season start based on similar consistency scores and tracks.
Each week Ivy publishes a leaderboard — which team has the higher collective follow-through percentage.
End of season: the winning Circle's wallets receive a bonus top-up funded by Ivy.
The losing Circle's Season Close call includes a rematch offer for Season 2.

The viral moment: the weekly leaderboard drop. Trash talk between Circles on social is free marketing.

Activity Concept 5 — The Streak Heist
A sabotage mechanic. Once per season, any user can challenge another user to a 7-day streak battle with wallet stakes.

You challenge someone — both users commit to 7 consecutive days on their chosen habit.
First to break their streak loses their wallet contribution for that week to the other person's charity.
Ivy calls both users with extra intensity during the battle week.
Each user can only be challenged once per season.

The wallet money never leaves the charitable ecosystem — it shifts between charities. But the competitive stakes feel real and personal.

Activity Badges
Every completed activity earns a badge that lives on the user's profile and Season Summary. At Season Close, Ivy surfaces every activity the user participated in and completed. Badges are shareable — they are the social-media-friendly output of the Season Activities experience.

Build Sequencing — Which Activity Ships First
Of the five Season Activities, The Baton should ship first. It is the most buildable, the most viral, and sits cleanly on top of the existing daily call infrastructure. The failure state — the baton drop notification — is an immediate, shareable, emotionally charged moment that requires no new infrastructure beyond the call and logging system already in place.

5. Rivalry Seasons — Football & Tribal Identity
A Rivalry Season is a special season variant where Circles are formed around existing tribal identities — football clubs, universities, or other communities — and matched against their natural rivals. The competition structure is identical to a standard season, but the emotional stakes are completely different.

Why the football rivalry mechanic is bigger than it looks
Football fans already have the strongest tribal identity in the world.
You do not need to manufacture rivalry — it exists before Ivy touches it.
Arsenal vs Man United fans do not need to be told to compete.
Ivy gives that energy somewhere healthy and purposeful to go.
It also solves the cold start problem for Circles — tribal identity makes groups feel immediate.


How a Rivalry Season Works
Rivalry Seasons are announced in advance with a start date — users opt in by declaring their club.
Circles are formed within clubs — 6-8 fans of the same team per Circle.
Each Circle is matched against a rival club's Circle for the full 12-week season.
A rivalry leaderboard tracks collective consistency across all Circles from each club — so it is not just your Circle vs theirs, it is every Arsenal Circle vs every Man United Circle globally.
At Season Close, the winning club's collective stats are published: Arsenal fans were 12 percent more consistent than Man United fans this season.

The Press Release Moment
That final stat — which club's fans were most consistent — is a press release. Sports media picks it up. It is free marketing at scale that no paid campaign could replicate. Every season produces a new headline, a new talking point, a new reason for sports journalists to write about Ivy.

Sponsorship Vehicle
Rivalry Seasons are a natural sponsorship asset. A kit manufacturer, a sports nutrition brand, or a wellness-adjacent partner would pay to be associated with a Rivalry Season. The pitch is clean: a brand that sponsors Arsenal vs Man United consistency data is not gambling-adjacent or ethically compromised — it is positive, health-focused, and genuinely newsworthy. Sponsorship revenue from Rivalry Seasons is incremental to subscription revenue and does not affect the wallet pass-through model.

Track Considerations
Fitness and Focus translate most naturally to rivalry framing — Arsenal fans trained harder than Spurs fans is immediately funny and shareable.
Sleep and Balance are harder to frame as rivalry without feeling odd — these tracks work better as personal season goals within the rivalry context.
Rivalry Seasons work best as Fitness-first with other tracks optional.

Launch Sequencing — When Rivalry Seasons Become Viable
The press release moment only works if the stats are meaningful. 12 Arsenal users vs 11 Man United users is not a headline. Rivalry Seasons require sufficient user density per club to produce credible data and compelling leaderboards.

Minimum viable threshold: 500+ active users per club before launching a Rivalry Season publicly.
Soft launch: run a private Rivalry Season beta with a single fixture (e.g. Arsenal vs Spurs) once 200+ users per club are active — test the mechanics, gather data, build the story.
Public launch: announce the first official Rivalry Season as a PR moment — press outreach to sports and wellness media simultaneously.
Rivalry Seasons are a Year 1 end feature at the earliest, not a launch feature. Build the infrastructure early, activate when density is there.



6. Ivy Circles — Sprint Cohort Coaching
Ivy Circles is the structured group accountability feature available from Ivy Plus and Concierge tiers. It solves the isolation problem — Ivy holds users accountable daily, but Circles makes the journey social, witnessed, and coached.

Sprint Structure
Sprint length: 4 weeks (aligns with billing cycle; long enough to show meaningful progress).
Group size: 6-8 people maximum (small enough for everyone to speak, large enough for energy).
Frequency: One group session per sprint (monthly).

Cohort Grouping Criteria
Track alignment: Fitness users with Fitness users, Focus with Focus, etc.
Consistency score: Similar Ivy follow-through rates grouped together (prevents demotivation gaps).
Sprint number: Users in their first sprint stay together. By Sprint 3 they know each other — bonds form.

7. Ivy Impact Stories
Most charitable giving is abstract. You donate, money moves, and you never feel it land. Ivy Impact Stories closes that loop. Every sprint, members who hit their consistency threshold receive a personalised message from the charity their wallet funded, acknowledging their contribution by name.

This transforms a passive donation into a felt relationship. A voice saying your name is not forgettable. That moment becomes the emotional anchor that drives renewal, upgrades, and word-of-mouth.

The core idea
You follow through for 4 weeks. Money moves to your chosen charity.
At the end of the sprint, someone at that charity says thank you - specifically to you.
Not a newsletter. Not a generic receipt. Something with your name on it.
This is the moment that makes Ivy feel different to anything else in this space.

Operational Model
Ivy cannot manually coordinate personalised content with every charity for every member. Two models handle this at scale:

Model A - Charity Content Library (preferred)
As part of Ivy charity onboarding, charities agree to provide a content library: a bank of thank you notes, photos, audio clips, and video messages.
Ivy personalises the content at delivery - inserting the member name, consistency score, wallet amount, and impact data before sending.
The charity provides content once. Ivy handles personalisation and delivery at scale.
Content library refreshed quarterly - new stories, new faces, new moments.

Model B - Ivy-Produced Impact Updates (fallback)
For charities that cannot produce content, Ivy creates templated impact updates using the charity published data and branding.
Personalised with member name, consistency stats, and wallet contribution amount.
Lower emotional resonance than a real voice or video - but far more meaningful than a generic donation receipt.
Used as a bridge while the charity relationship matures toward Model A.

Authenticity is Non-Negotiable
Audio and video must feature real beneficiaries or coordinators - never AI-generated voices or stock footage.
Notes must reference specific, verifiable impact tied to the member wallet amount - not generic charity copy.
Concierge-tier video content must be genuinely personalised - a sign with the member name, a direct address, something that could not have been sent to anyone else.
Ivy audits charity content libraries quarterly to ensure quality and freshness.

Viral Potential
A member who receives a video of a child holding a sign with their name is likely to share it. That organic moment is more powerful than any paid marketing campaign. Impact Stories should be designed with shareability in mind: one-tap sharing to social, a clean Ivy-branded frame, and a caption that tells the story without requiring explanation.

12. Strategic Notes
Pricing Philosophy — Full AI, Human as Differentiator
Every paid tier includes the complete AI experience. No rescue calls, tracks, or features are gated behind higher tiers.

The two honest differentiators are: (1) wallet size — more money to charity — and (2) human involvement quality — peer coach vs. certified pro vs. celebrity.
Human involvement is naturally scarce and expensive — it is a genuine differentiator, not an artificial one.
The one-liner: "Every Ivy plan gives you the full AI experience. What you are choosing is how much impact you want to create and how much human coaching you want alongside it."

14-Day Free Trial Rationale
Ivy's value proposition is the daily call. A permanently crippled free tier (no calls) misrepresents the product and creates negative first impressions.
A 14-day trial with the full Ivy experience — real calls, real wallet, real Ivy — converts far better than a feature-limited freemium tier.
"Try Ivy free for 14 days. No card required. Real calls, real impact from day one." is a cleaner, more premium pitch.

vs. fitness apps (MyFitnessPal, Strava): Ivy is not a tracking app. It is an accountability layer that sits on top of any routine.
vs. human coaches (£200-500/mo): Ivy delivers comparable accountability at £70-149/mo without judgment anxiety.

13. Memorial Season & Tribute Season
Two of Ivy's most emotionally significant features sit at the intersection of accountability, grief, and human connection. Neither requires meaningful extra build — both require exceptional care in execution. Together they position Ivy as a product that shows up at the moments that matter most.

The Memorial Season — Individual
A Memorial Season is a season dedicated to someone who has died. The user sets their goal in that person's name. Their Impact Wallet donation goes to a charity chosen in their honour. At the Season Close, Ivy delivers a ceremonial call that weaves the user's transformation data with the memory of who they were doing it for.

Onboarding variation: a single additional question — who are you dedicating this season to — with an optional name, relationship, and memory.
Charity selection is reframed: choose a cause in their name.
The daily call opens with quiet acknowledgement: you are showing up for someone who mattered. That is enough.
The Season Close is the most significant call Ivy ever makes for that user — their arc, their data, their wallet total, and a space to remember the person by name.

Why this is almost free to build
A Memorial Season is a season variant with a different onboarding flow and modified call scripts.
The infrastructure is identical to a standard season.
The only real work is the call script — which requires care, not engineering.
The emotional impact and organic virality make this worth doing in the first product cycle.


The Tribute Season — Circle Level
A Tribute Season is a Memorial Season undertaken collectively by an entire Circle. When a Circle member loses someone, the group may vote unanimously to dedicate the full season to that person's memory. If the vote passes, the season transforms.

The season goal is collectively reframed: we are running for David. We are showing up every day for her.
Every member's Impact Wallet for that season directs to a single charity chosen in the name of the person who passed.
The daily call acknowledges the dedication: you are in a Tribute Season. Every day you show up, you are honouring someone who mattered.
Sprint Circle sessions include a dedicated segment — a moment of acknowledgement, a shared memory, a collective intention set.
Impact Stories at sprint end are addressed to the Circle collectively. The charity knows this group dedicated 12 weeks to someone's memory.
The Tribute Close at Season 12 surfaces the full collective arc, the total donated, and a space to remember the person together.

Why the Tribute Season is More Powerful Than the Individual Version
Grief is isolating. One of the hardest things about losing someone is that life around you keeps moving. A Circle that collectively dedicates a season to a member's loss says: we are pausing with you. We are doing this together, for them. That is something almost nothing in the world offers.

A Circle that goes through a Tribute Season together has been through something real. Those users do not churn. They recruit. They tell the story at the person's anniversary, at the next funeral, to anyone who has ever lost someone and not known what to do with it.

Safeguards and Sensitivity Requirements
The loss must always be the member's choice to share — Ivy never surfaces or references it unless explicitly chosen.
The vote to dedicate a season must be unanimous and introduced gently, never prominently.
The daily call tone during a Tribute Season must be quiet acknowledgement, not grief counselling. The register is: you showed up today. That matters.
Privacy is absolute — a member's reason for a Memorial Season is never visible to others without explicit consent.
Missed days during a Memorial Season require a different call register. The normal nudge logic is too blunt for someone running a season in a loved one's memory who has had a hard week. When a user in a Memorial Season misses a day, Ivy's call script shifts to quiet acknowledgement only: grief is not linear. Showing up when you can is enough. No pressure. No streak urgency. Just presence.

Partnership Opportunities
Hospices and palliative care networks — Ivy as a recommended tool for bereaved family members channelling grief into purposeful action.
Bereavement charities — Marie Curie, Cruse Bereavement Support — as charity wallet partners with natural alignment.
NHS grief pathway programmes — Tribute Seasons as a structured post-bereavement activity with measurable outcomes.

15. The During Layer
Ivy is currently present before and after every habit. The morning call sets the commitment. The evening call closes the loop. But the actual workout, the jog, the meditation, the revision block — Ivy is absent for that. The user is alone. The During Layer is the Phase 2 initiative that changes this.

The vision for the During Layer originated from thinking about real-time spatial features — users seeing themselves racing against others as colour-coded dots on a live map, competing against their personal best, drone escort coverage of a special run generating cinematic footage, and eventually an AR version of yourself racing against your past self should AR glasses become mainstream. These ideas are directionally right and worth naming as a long-term destination. The During Layer roadmap below sequences them from immediately buildable to genuinely futuristic.

Phase 1 — Running Together (No GPS Required)
Most of what makes running with others feel different is psychological, not spatial. You do not need a live map to create the feeling of shared presence. Ivy can announce at the start of a workout: three people in your Circle are also training today. Sarah started 20 minutes ago. You are running together. No GPS sync, no multiplayer infrastructure — just the psychological reality of shared effort, which is enough to change the experience.

Phase 2 — Live Audio Companion During Workouts
When a user starts their workout, they can tap “run with Ivy.” Ivy joins them via earpiece — not as a constant talker, but as a calibrated presence. She speaks occasionally and precisely, drawing on personal data to know when to intervene. At 12 minutes in, if that is the user’s usual drop-off point, Ivy says something. At the halfway mark, Ivy acknowledges it. When the session ends, Ivy closes the loop before the evening call even fires.

This is a Retell audio session — infrastructure already built. The personalisation layer (knowing when the user typically drops off, what motivates them, what their current season goal is) comes from existing data. Build complexity is low. Emotional impact is high.

Phase 2 — Personal Best Ghost
Show the user their own pace from their best previous run overlaid on their current session — a ghost of their best self that they are racing against. Not other users, not a target set by an algorithm — just themselves vs their own best. Simple GPS plus stored pace data. No multiplayer infrastructure. This sits in the app as a Fitness track feature and ships in Phase 2 once the core loop is proven.

Phase 3 — Live Map Racing
Real-time multiplayer location sync — Circle members appear as colour-coded dots on a live map as they run, walk, or cycle simultaneously. The Baton activity mechanic gets a visual layer. Rivalry Season runs become genuinely competitive in real time. This requires GPS tracking infrastructure, a map rendering layer (Mapbox or Google Maps SDK), and real-time sync architecture. It is a meaningful engineering investment and ships in Phase 3 once the social layer is proven at scale.

Phase 3 — Drone Escort (Concierge Add-On)
A drone escort for a significant run — a first marathon, a Season Close workout, a Memorial Season tribute run, a Rivalry Season finale. The user books it as a Concierge add-on (small per-run fee), a local drone operator is dispatched, cinematic footage is captured and delivered. This is a concierge logistics service, not a software feature. It requires a drone operator marketplace, insurance and airspace compliance, and video delivery infrastructure. The emotional concept is exactly right — a Season Close run deserves to look like something. The operational model needs careful design before committing to build.

Phase 4 — AR Self Racing (Long-Term Vision)
As AR glasses become mainstream — Apple Vision Pro, Meta Ray-Bans, and their successors — the personal best ghost mechanic becomes a full AR rendering. You see a translucent version of yourself from your best season running alongside you in real space. The data model that powers this is being built from day one: every run, every pace, every GPS trace is stored. The AR layer reads from that history. No new data infrastructure required at Phase 4 — just a rendering layer on top of years of accumulated personal performance data.

16. The Social Layer — Outward Mechanics
Ivy's existing social features — Circles, Rivalry Seasons, Season Activities — keep energy inside the platform. The features in this section are different. They make Ivy's emotional reality legible to people who have never heard of it, and they give existing users reasons to pull others in without ever feeling like they are selling something.

The positioning foundation
Most social platforms are built on performance — you post when you are ready, you curate the highlight. Ivy is structurally the opposite: proactive, unscheduled, voice-native, financially verified. This makes Ivy uniquely positioned to become the first social platform built on verified vulnerability — where the social currency is authentic struggle and growth, not polish. Every mechanic in this section expresses the same idea: Ivy makes real things real in a world full of fake things. That is not a feature. It is a positioning moat that compounds as the world grows more sceptical of curated social media.


The Witness Mechanic
Every Ivy user can nominate one Witness — a partner, a friend, a parent, a mentor — who does not need to download anything or create an account. Each week, the Witness receives a plain-text email: Michael said he'd train four times this week. He trained four times. £15 went to Mind on his behalf. That is the entire message.

The Witness can reply with a voice note. Ivy reads it to the user on their next morning call.

Why this changes everything
It creates genuine interpersonal stakes without requiring both parties to be users.
The Witness feels the weight of what the user is doing — and that feeling is what makes them want their own Season.
It is a zero-friction referral loop disguised entirely as a product feature. No invite link. No sign-up prompt. Just a weekly email that lands and stays.
The Accountability Buddy feature already built is the functional foundation — the Witness is its evolved, outward-facing form.

Build complexity: very low. Extends existing Accountability Buddies infrastructure. Ships at launch.


The Season Card
At Season Close, Ivy generates a shareable artefact — a 60-second audio-visual summary of the user's full 12-week arc.

What it contains
Consistency percentage across the season.
Voice sentiment arc — were they more energised at week 8 than week 2? The data exists in every call.
Total donated to charity across the season.
One AI-selected highlight moment: the most honest, most triumphant spoken check-in from the entire season, surfaced and played back.
Total wallet impact in plain language: your follow-through funded 12 school meals, 4 mosquito nets, 3 weeks of counselling.

The Season Card is shareable to LinkedIn, Instagram Stories, or anywhere. It comes with an Ivy-branded frame and a caption that tells the story without needing explanation.

Why this is the anti-influencer social post
Instagram is performative. LinkedIn is curated. The Season Card is the opposite — raw, real, earned, and verified by financial stakes. No other platform can produce this artefact. A user cannot fake a Season Card. That authenticity is its entire value as social proof.
Every Season Close becomes a distribution event. A user sharing their Season Card is the most credible possible advertisement Ivy will ever have.

Build complexity: low-medium. Audio-visual generation layer on top of existing Season data. Phase 2.


The Pact Mechanic
Two users make a shared commitment. Both check in on the same habit or goal. If either breaks their streak, both lose their Impact Wallet contribution for that week — and it redirects to the other person's chosen charity.

Not competing. Co-responsible. The emotional dynamic is completely different from rivalry — it is closer to a vow than a bet. I am not letting you down because if I do, your money goes to someone else's cause.

Use cases
Couples working on a shared health goal.
Co-founders keeping a morning routine promise.
Close friends doing something hard at the same time.
A parent and adult child building a habit together.

Design note: the Pact is deliberately asymmetric to avoid surveillance — neither person can see the other's exact call transcript. They see follow-through or not. That boundary is important.

The Pact is the lightweight Phase 2 wedge for Concept C — Relationship Accountability. It tests whether users want Ivy for interpersonal commitments before building the full product.

Build complexity: low. Shared commitment linking on existing data model. Phase 2.


The Ghost Mechanic
When a user abandons their Season — stops responding, cancels, disappears — their remaining Impact Wallet balance auto-donates in full to their chosen charity. Their Witness (if they have one) receives a message: [Name]'s Season ended early — but £X went to Shelter in their name.

You failed with grace. The money still moved. The charity still benefited. The social cost of quitting is real but not humiliating.

Why dignified failure is a growth mechanic
This is counter-intuitive but important: lowering the perceived cost of quitting increases the willingness to start. The biggest acquisition barrier for any accountability product is the fear of public failure. The Ghost mechanic removes that fear. You will not be humiliated if you don't finish — you will be honoured for what you contributed while you were here.
That framing makes Ivy safe to try. And users who feel safe to try are users who come back for Season 2.

Build complexity: very low. Wallet auto-donation on cancellation event. Ships at launch.
